Whole-Home Renovation

Multi-room and full property renovation, planned and built as one project.

A whole-home renovation is a different exercise from a series of room projects. The rooms are connected, the systems run between them, and a decision made in the kitchen changes what is possible in the bathroom on the other side of the wall.

Run as separate jobs, that coordination falls to the homeowner. Run as one project, it is our problem rather than yours.

One team from start to finish

We plan, design, permit, source and build. That matters more at this scale than at any other, because the number of dependencies grows faster than the number of rooms.

Electrical and plumbing are laid out for the finished house, not room by room. Flooring runs are planned as continuous surfaces rather than meeting at awkward thresholds. Long-lead items are ordered against a schedule that accounts for every trade, not just the one working that week.

Sequencing a whole house

The order is not negotiable, and understanding it changes how the project feels to live through.

Structural work comes first, then rough-in for plumbing, electrical and HVAC throughout the property, then inspections, then walls close. That middle stretch is long and produces almost nothing you can photograph. It is also the phase that determines whether everything after it works.

Once walls are closed the house changes quickly — drywall, then tile and waterproofing in wet areas, then cabinetry, then the gap while stone counters are templated and fabricated, then finishes. The final ten percent takes longer than the near-finished rooms suggest it should.

Older properties

Much of San Diego County's housing stock was built to standards that have since moved on. Opening a whole house tends to reveal more than opening one room: knob-and-tube remnants, undersized panels, framing altered by previous owners, walls that are neither square nor plumb.

We would rather build the assessment into the plan than discover it halfway. Where we can look before demolition, we do.

Staying or moving out

Some clients live in part of the house while we work on the rest. Others move out for the duration. Both work, and the decision changes how we phase the job — a lived-in renovation is sequenced to keep a kitchen, a bathroom and a clear route between them functioning at all times.

Tell us which you intend and we will build the schedule around it rather than around the ideal case.

Common Questions

Questions We Are Asked

Do I need to move out during a whole-home renovation?

Both approaches work. Some clients live in part of the house while we work on the rest; others move out for the duration. Tell us which you intend and we phase the work around it — a lived-in renovation is sequenced to keep a kitchen, a bathroom and a clear route between them functioning throughout.

Why is there a long stretch where nothing seems to happen?

That is the rough-in phase, when plumbing, electrical and HVAC go in throughout the property before walls close. It produces almost nothing you can photograph and it determines whether everything after it works. Once walls close, the house changes quickly.

Do you coordinate all the trades yourself?

Yes. One schedule, one sequence, one company accountable. On a project this size the number of dependencies grows faster than the number of rooms, and coordination is most of the job.

What do you find in older San Diego homes?

Opening a whole house tends to reveal more than opening one room — undersized electrical panels, framing altered by previous owners, walls that are neither square nor plumb. Where we can look before demolition, we do, so it goes into the plan rather than surfacing halfway through.
