Outdoor Construction

Decks, patio covers, pergolas, pavers and artificial turf built for the San Diego climate.

Outdoor space in San Diego is usable most of the year, which is exactly why it is worth building properly. The conditions here are specific: strong ultraviolet exposure, salt in the air well inland, and a daily cycle of damp marine mornings giving way to dry afternoons. That cycle is harder on timber and hardware than steady weather in either direction.

Decks

We build in composite, PVC and timber. Which one suits you comes down to a question worth answering honestly: how much maintenance will you actually do?

Capped composite and PVC need no refinishing and do not splinter. Redwood and cedar give you something engineered boards still cannot quite reproduce, in exchange for cleaning and refinishing on a cycle set by sun exposure, which is a commitment of time rather than material. Tropical hardwood lasts decades and wants oiling.

What matters as much as the boards is what sits under and behind them. Framing, footings, the flashing where a deck meets the house, and how water sheds away rather than sitting against the structure. Near the coast, fasteners and connectors deserve real attention — corrosion there is not cosmetic, and it is invisible until it is not.

Patio covers

The first question is how much sky you want to keep. A solid roof gives full shade and rain protection and turns a patio into an outdoor room, though it darkens whatever sits behind it. Lattice and open slat filter the sun and cast a pattern that moves through the day. Louvered systems adjust.

Orientation should drive the choice more than appearance does. A west-facing patio takes low, hard afternoon sun that an overhead structure barely touches — those spaces usually need something vertical on the sun side. We would rather work that out on site than on a plan.

Because a cover is a ceiling, it is the natural place for lighting, fans, heaters and power. All of it is straightforward to run during construction and awkward afterwards.

Pavers, hardscape and turf

Pavers and artificial turf have become the default pairing for yards here, and combining them well is mostly geometry — plus a base nobody ever sees.

A paver installation is a system: excavation to depth, compacted aggregate base, bedding layer, pavers, jointing sand, and an edge restraint holding the field in place. Pavers that sink, rock underfoot or drift apart are reporting a base problem, not a paver problem.

Paving also changes where water goes. Surfaces fall away from the house, at a slope you cannot feel underfoot but which moves water reliably. Where a paved area sits against a wall that blocks runoff, drainage becomes part of the design rather than an afterthought.

Planned as one project

Decks, covers, hardscape and the utilities running to them are designed together and built by one team. Conduit for lighting, a gas line for a future fire feature, power for a kitchen — all far easier to stub in before the base goes down than to cut into finished work later.

Common Questions

Questions We Are Asked

Composite or wood — which should I choose?

It comes down to how much maintenance you will genuinely do. Capped composite and PVC need no refinishing and do not splinter. Redwood and cedar give you something engineered boards cannot quite reproduce, in exchange for cleaning and refinishing on a cycle set by sun exposure.

Will a composite deck get hot underfoot?

Any engineered board absorbs more heat than timber, and colour drives it more than material does. A dark board in full afternoon sun gets genuinely hot. If people will be barefoot on it, that is an argument for a lighter colour, and some manufacturers make lines formulated to run cooler.

Solid roof or open lattice for a patio cover?

Decide whether you want rain protection or only sun control — that single answer removes most of the options. A solid roof makes the space usable in any weather but darkens the rooms behind it. Lattice filters sun and keeps the light. Orientation matters more than appearance: a west-facing patio takes low afternoon sun that an overhead structure barely touches.

Why do pavers sink or come loose over time?

Almost always the base rather than the pavers. A paver installation is a system — excavation to depth, compacted aggregate base, bedding layer, jointing sand, and an edge restraint holding the field in place. Without proper edge restraint the outer courses migrate and the joints open up.

Does building near the coast change anything?

Considerably. Salt air reaches well inland and is hard on metal, so fasteners and connectors deserve real attention — that hardware holds the structure together and is invisible once built. The daily cycle of damp mornings and dry afternoons is also harder on timber than steady conditions.
