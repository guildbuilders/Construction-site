Building in La Mesa

La Mesa has an older, established housing stock — a good deal of it mid-century, much of it on hillside lots, and a fair amount with genuine architectural character.

Age brings the usual realities. Walls that are not square, framing modified by previous owners, plumbing and electrical extended over decades rather than replaced. Cabinetry and tile in a home like this need scribing and careful setting out, and we would rather uncover what is behind a wall and tell you than close it back up.

Slope shapes outdoor work. A deck that steps down a grade needs far less structure than one platform built out over the fall, and level changes separate spaces without blocking sightlines. On a graded lot, where water goes after a paved area is added has to be planned rather than assumed.

Mid-century homes reward restraint. Where the original character is worth keeping, the work is about making the house function properly without stripping what makes it distinctive.
