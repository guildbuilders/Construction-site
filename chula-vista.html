Building in Chula Vista

Chula Vista splits fairly clearly between older established neighbourhoods and the large master-planned developments to the east, and the work differs between them.

In the older parts of the city, homes from the mid-twentieth century still carry original kitchens and bathrooms in many cases. Those layouts reflect how households worked then, and the opportunity is usually in changing the plan rather than refinishing it. Opening those walls reveals what previous work left behind, which we would rather find early than late.

The newer eastern developments are a different exercise. Framing is consistent, walls are square, and what is behind them is predictable. The work there is about lifting a builder-grade house to something better fitted — proper cabinetry, lighting planned in layers, bathrooms with storage designed rather than added.

Yards in the newer neighbourhoods were often handed over bare. Pavers, turf, decks and patio covers make them usable, and in this climate they get used nearly year-round.
