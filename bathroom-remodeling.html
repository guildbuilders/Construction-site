Bathroom Remodeling

Full bathroom remodels, shower and tub conversions, tile and vanity work across San Diego County.

Bathrooms are the smallest rooms most people remodel and the least forgiving. Everything is packed tight, water is present on every surface, and the assembly that keeps that water out of the structure is buried where nobody will ever look at it again.

That last point is worth dwelling on. Tile and grout are not waterproof — water passes through grout. What protects the framing behind your shower is the membrane and detailing underneath, and it is invisible the moment tile goes on. It is the part of a bathroom we will happily talk you through in detail, and a fair thing to ask any contractor about.

How we run a bathroom

We handle the project from first measurement to final walkthrough: layout and design, permitting, ordering fixtures and materials, and every stage of construction. One team, one schedule.

Bathrooms reward decisions made early, because the work is so tightly sequenced. The position of a shower valve, whether a niche is lit, where blocking goes for a future grab bar — all of it is set while the walls are open and disruptive to revisit afterwards. We ask those questions sooner than feels necessary, for that reason.

What we build

Full remodels. Down to studs and back, including moving fixtures where the layout warrants it. Relocating a toilet is a larger undertaking than moving a vanity, and we will tell you which of your changes are straightforward and which are not.

Shower and tub conversions. Removing an unused tub for a larger walk-in shower is the most requested change we see. Curbless entries, frameless glass, benches and recessed niches all get resolved at the framing stage rather than improvised later.

Tile and stone. Layout is set out before anything is fixed, so cuts land where they should and the pattern resolves cleanly at edges and corners. Metal trim on exposed edges is the detail that most separates custom work from a standard tile job.

Vanities, fixtures and lighting. Light at the mirror positioned at face height rather than overhead. Storage recessed into the wall where floor area is tight. Ventilation sized for the room and vented outside, not into an attic.

Older homes

A good share of the bathrooms we open in San Diego County turn up something the previous work left behind. Plumbing run in a way nobody would specify now, framing modified without support, moisture damage that was tiled over rather than repaired.

We would rather find it and tell you than close the wall on it. When something is uncovered, you hear about it the same day, with what it means for the scope and the schedule.

Common Questions

Questions We Are Asked

What actually keeps water out of the walls?

The waterproofing assembly behind the tile, not the tile itself. Grout is not waterproof — water passes through it. What protects the framing is the membrane and detailing underneath, installed before any tile goes on. We are happy to walk you through how we build a shower, and it is a fair question to ask any contractor.

Should I remove the tub for a larger shower?

It is the most requested change we see, and it usually transforms how a small bathroom works. Worth considering first: whether you bathe small children, and whether the house retains a tub somewhere else. If it is the only bathroom in the home, the decision deserves more caution.

Can you move the toilet or the shower?

Yes, though not all fixtures are equally simple to relocate. Moving a vanity is straightforward. Relocating a toilet is a larger undertaking because of the waste line and the fall it needs. We will tell you which of your changes are simple and which are not before you commit to a layout.

What happens if you find damage once the walls are open?

You hear about it the same day, with what it means for scope and schedule. Older homes frequently turn up something — plumbing run in a way nobody would specify now, or moisture damage that was tiled over rather than repaired. We would rather find it than close the wall on it.

Do you handle fixtures and materials, or do I order them?

We source and sequence everything. Ordering is not just buying — it is knowing what has a long lead time and starting it early enough that the schedule is built around real availability.
